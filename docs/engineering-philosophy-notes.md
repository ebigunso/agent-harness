# Notes: the engineering philosophy's discussion

Unratified. Counsel's notes for the discussion of an engineering philosophy for this repository. No philosophy is drafted yet. No entry here is grounds for a plan or a grade, and none is his answer; his words are quoted so that a later Counsel session can continue from them.

## What he has said, 2026-11-11

- Decision, his, on the base: he wants the engineering philosophy written, based on the one he ratified for another repository of his, with what was found during the work in this repository added to it.
- Decision, his, on what lies under it: "I have a feeling that the current iteration ... actually rests on a deeper underlying idea: strive to increase the value of the repository's contents. Value would appear as they are in many forms, but the underlying thing is that what is valuable is what survives the test of time. You won't be ever sure of what actually would survive, so the thing to strive for is to go for whatever that you think at the time would have the best chance of surviving the test of time, without it requiring new changes very soon."

## Counsel's take, its own, put to him 2026-11-11

- The idea is real and it is under his directions in this repository as much as under the base: records "expected to hold through subsequent changes" (2026-11-02), wording "less likely to change meaning when something else changes" (2026-11-07), reasons in place of model names, a Why that gives reasons of its own.
- Three things the idea needs before it can stand first:
  1. Surviving is not the same as being valuable. The base's own central point is that the wrong things survive best, because everything added gets pinned. So the test is surviving a later reader who is free to remove it, not staying because nobody dared.
  2. Aiming at what lasts must not become building for imagined futures. What lasts is what makes the fewest bets, and makes them on what is written down, not on guesses.
  3. Some content is meant to change. The failure is content that has to change though nothing outside it did.
- What the work here would add, as Counsel sees it: content that changes fast is kept apart from content that changes slowly and is dated; a statement names the thing itself; a judge is never the party under pressure; text written for a capable reader states intent and reasons; the person's attention is spent only where nothing within reach answers.

## His answers, 2026-11-11

- On which reading of "survives" he means: "Those free to refactor would not face the need or want to overhaul."
- On whether the idea stands first or beneath as the reason: "Philosophy first."

- Fact: sections 8 to 12 are carried from the base with the parts about that product removed. Removed and not replaced: the behaviour standard and its scenes; the rule that behaviour is read by a person against a control; "quality repaired after the fact". Each is about that product.

- Departures from the conventional answer, for him to see before ratifying: the aim is what stands, where the conventional aim is correctness, readability or speed of change; removing is treated as the safe act and adding as the irreversible one; text for agents states intent and reasons where the convention is exhaustive procedure; the person is asked less, not more.

## His challenge and Counsel's examination, 2026-11-11

- His words: "I want you to examine the cases I made, instead of taking them at face value. Do they actually serve what I want?"
- Counsel's examination, its own, of his three cases (the aim, the test, the aim standing first). The draft took them as given and overclaimed for them; these are the findings put to him:
  1. Tested against his own decisions of 2026-11-10 and 11, the aim predicts a minority. It predicts the ones about records and wording (split a record, name the thing, reasons in place of names). It does not predict the ones about his attention and waiting (rewrites go in unread, ask only where nothing within reach settles it, research never waits), about people who use the harness (Counsel as the default because people will not choose), or about cost. Those have other roots.
  2. The draft's sentence "Everything below is this aim applied" is false. Sections on authority, independent judgement and his attention do not follow from what lasts.
  3. An agent told to choose what will last reaches for the defaults the base forbids: guards, generality, compatibility. Standing first, the aim is what an agent applies to the case no section names, which is where that pull is strongest.
  4. "What survives is valuable" also reads as "what exists has earned its place", against the base's rule that the shape of what exists has no claim.
  5. The test is passed trivially by later agents, who seldom want to overhaul anything; and its word "need" counts changes forced from outside as failures, which invites building for imagined futures.
  6. Value has no "to whom" in the text. The base leans on a product philosophy for that; this repository has none.
- Counsel's proposal, its own: keep the aim, narrowed to the one thing it explains (how contents are written so they need no rewriting), carrying its direction in the same sentence (what needs least assumes least); name the other roots beside it; make the test a question the author asks at the time of writing, with outside changes excluded; keep "favour the design that needs less" as the tie-break; and take the missing "to whom" to a product philosophy.

## The search for the underlying principle, opened 2026-11-11

- His words: "If there are so many issues that crop up, my read on what the underlying principal was could have been wrong. I'd like to discuss with you to find out what that actually is. Ask me questions where needed, and add your insights where relevant."
- Candidates Counsel put to him, its own, each tested against his decisions:
  1. What lasts (his first read). Explains his directions on records and wording; not the ones on his attention, on waiting, or on people who use the harness.
  2. Nobody has to come back to it. Work done so that no one, the person, the next agent or a user, has to return to it: not rewrite it, not read it again, not be asked again, not wait on it. Lasting would be one case of this.
  3. Reasons over instructions. Each thing carries its reason at the level where the reason lives, so a capable reader can decide the case nobody listed; what cannot give a reason goes. Lasting would be a result of this.
- Questions put to him: whether the replaced records would have bothered him had he never had to read the replacements; which is worse to find a year on, a rule nobody can explain that is still in force, or a well-reasoned decision replaced because the world changed; and which lines of the base he feels most strongly about.

## His answers to the three questions, 2026-11-11

- On whether replaced records would have bothered him had he never had to read the replacements: "Well, that is as much of an issue as if I had to see them. I think you've mixed up two things here, how repository content quality is held, which should go to the engineering philosophy, and how the harness should assume lazy humans as the default and aim to reduce human churn where possible, which probably is more of a product philosophy line."

What follows from them, Counsel's reading:

- Decision, his: the engineering philosophy is about how the quality of the repository's contents is held. His attention, waiting, and people who will not choose belong to a product philosophy, and so do the candidate "nobody has to come back to it" and most of the draft's section 6.
- Churn in the contents is a fault in itself, whoever does or does not read it. A decision replaced because the world changed is acceptable; a rule nobody can explain is much worse than that. So lasting is not the root: a reason is.
- Candidate Counsel put to him next, its own: everything in the repository answers to a reason one level up, states it, and changes only when that reason does. Clean is nothing present without such a reason; the hierarchy is where the reasons live; lasting is what results. Insight offered with it: the hierarchy is also an ordering by how fast reasons change, and content placed at the wrong level changes at the wrong rate.
- Questions put to him next: whether a piece with a sound reason in the right place that is clumsily made is clean; and what stands at the top of the chain in this repository, where no product philosophy exists.

## His answers on a clumsy piece and on the top of the chain, 2026-11-11

- On whether a piece with a sound reason in the right place that is confusingly made is clean: "It depends on where that resides. If that is something meant for humans to read and judge, confusing content hinders the ability to make informed decisions, so that is bad. On the other hand, if that complexity lives in places where only agents read and write, if that complexity is actually the right shape considering how it should work and how it would evolve, then that is fine. Confusing even in the agent's level of perception would be bad though. I think the important part is that the intent is clear enough when read in a void, that anyone can later work on it with confidence in what they are working with."

Counsel's reading, its own:

- Candidate as it now stands, put to him for confirmation: the repository is kept so that whoever works on it later, person or agent, starting cold, can act on any part of it with confidence, knowing what it is for, what it answers to, and when it would change or go. Clean and the hierarchy are the two means. Lasting, and a later reader finding nothing to overhaul, are what result.
