# Agent Harness Engineering Philosophy

**Whoever works on this repository later, person or agent, starting cold, can act on any part of it with confidence: they can tell what it is for, what it answers to, and when it would change or go.**

## 1. Where the value is

What this repository produces is not text. It is what agents do, in other people's repositories, because they read it. A sentence in the harness is worth the behaviour it changes for the better, less what it costs every agent that has to read it. A sentence that changes nothing has a cost and no worth.

A capable agent already does most things well. So the value sits in a small number of places: where its instinct would take it the wrong way, where two good things pull apart and it would pick the one nobody can blame, where it needs to know what something is for in order to handle the case nobody wrote down, and where there is a line that holds whatever else is true. Everything written into the harness is judged by whether it is one of those.

## 2. What it aspires to

**Documents that know what they are for.** Each document answers to something above it and adds one thing the one above does not hold, so a reader can judge it against what it answers to instead of against its own account of itself. When review finds something in every round, this is usually what is missing, and the cure is to lift the document back to what it is for, not to patch each gap.

**What someone meets, settled before the means.** A decision first says what a person does and what they then experience, and only then how that is brought about. The two are kept apart so that either can change without the other.

**Contents that say what is so.** A limit is written as a limit and not dressed as the behaviour that was wanted. A guess is not written as a finding. A fact that may change carries the date it was seen. A later worker can rely only on what is honest about its own standing.

## 3. What to state

- **What the thing is for.** Intent is what lets a reader decide the case that was never listed, and it is what a later worker needs first.
- **The reason.** A reader who has the reason can tell when the statement has stopped applying. A reader who has only the statement can obey it or drop it, and cannot do either with confidence.
- **Where instinct goes wrong.** Say so in those words, and name the comfortable default being refused. Agreement with what the reader would have done anyway is not worth its space.
- **Which good wins.** Where two goods conflict, the text settles it. Left unsettled, the choice goes to whichever option protects its author.
- **The line that holds.** A constraint that binds regardless is written as one, apart from advice.

## 4. What to leave open

- **The route.** Steps, methods and the order of work are the reader's. A reader told how stops asking whether.
- **The cases.** A list of cases is incomplete the day a new one appears; a reason covers it.
- **What the reader can see for itself.** How the repository is laid out, what the code does, what a file contains.
- **What changes faster than the place it would be written.** A model's name, a price, a count, a date-bound fact: these go where changing them costs one line.
- **Anything that would be matched instead of understood.** A named pattern, a template, an example that looks like a form, a metric. Once written, it is what readers aim at.

Leaving something open is a decision, and where a later writer would be tempted to fill the gap, the text says it was left open and why.

## 5. Where it goes

Each thing is written once, in the place whose pace matches its reason, where the reader who needs it will be reading when the decision comes.

A philosophy changes slowest. It holds what the work aspires to and the thinking behind it, including an aspiration whose bearing on any one decision cannot yet be seen: it is kept because it shows how the thinking goes, which is what a later reader needs for the decision nobody foresaw. A decision record holds one decision at the level of intent, so that the text beneath it can be reworded freely. The harness's text holds how decisions are carried out. A workspace's dated record holds what is true today. Something placed too high gets rewritten for reasons that were never its own; something placed too low is not there when it is needed. Contents that have to be replaced while their reason stands are a failure in themselves, because they teach every later reader not to rely on what is written.

## 6. For whom

Clear is measured for the reader a piece has. Where a person reads in order to judge, it is plain enough to decide from, because confusion there takes the decision away from them. Where only agents read and write, complexity is no fault if it is the right shape for how the thing works and how it will grow. What confuses its own reader is a fault anywhere.

## 7. What clean means

Clean has two senses and both are meant: nothing present that cannot say why it is there, and what is present doing its work with the least complexity, in a shape that takes the next thing of its kind without an overhaul. That shape comes from building along the lines on which the work really varies, which usually means fewer ideas. It does not come from parts added for cases nobody has asked for. What counts as the next thing is what is already written down as the direction, in the philosophies, the accepted decisions and the ratified briefs, and never a future someone can imagine.

Order is something to aspire to, and reshaping is how it is kept. A change that would be cleaner with its surroundings reshaped says so, instead of bending itself to fit. When it is in order, the repository reads as the work: someone who knows what the harness is for can predict where a thing is and what it is called before they look.

Three reversals of ordinary instinct carry most of it.

**Adding is the act that cannot be taken back.** What is added gets pinned by whatever is then built on it, and removing it later costs more than leaving it out now. So the burden of proof is on what is added or kept, and absence needs no defence.

**What already exists has no claim.** The shape of the surrounding contents is an accident of earlier changes. It is not an instruction, and fitting in with it is not a virtue. Nor has an imagined future a claim, until it is shown to arrive in ordinary use.

**The author's safety is not the project's.** A guard, a kept file, a preserved interface, an extra sentence of caution: nobody is blamed for these, which is why they accumulate. The cost is paid later by someone else. Clean work takes the small risk of being wrong now.

These cut the other way too. What a real need relies on is kept, and the need is written beside it so that nobody strips it for want of knowing. The harness's own text states the practice that follows from all this, and this philosophy does not repeat it.

## 8. How to tell

Hand any piece to someone who has only the piece and what it points to. If they can say what it is for, what it answers to and what would let it go, it is in order. If they have to guess, it is not, however long it has stood. A rule nobody can explain is the worst case: no one can act on it with confidence, so no one touches it.

A repository kept this way gives a later worker little reason to overhaul it. That is the sign that it is working and never the aim, since contents also last when nobody dares question them.

## 9. North star

> Could someone who comes to this cold tell what it is for and what would let it go; and does it change what a capable reader would otherwise have done?

When in doubt, write less, say what was left open, and leave the repository in better order than it was found.
