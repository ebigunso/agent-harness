# ADR-D (not yet numbered): The word of the person directing the work reaches an Orchestrator session as that person's own statement there or as Counsel's relay quoting that person; in a session Counsel started, that person's answer to the entry that admits relays is itself carried by Counsel

## Context and Problem Statement

Terms used here: the person directing the work is the one who says what the work should do and how the project should look, and judges what is built from its behaviour. Counsel is the session role that person talks with about what the product does or should do. The Orchestrator session is a session that holds the Orchestrator role and plans, dispatches and reports the work. A relay is Counsel's message to an Orchestrator session that quotes that person's words. The admitting entry is the standing approval in a repository's rule files that names Counsel's identity as the carrier of that person's decisions for the repository. A session's window is where a person would type into that session.

The person directing the work talks with Counsel, and an Orchestrator session's consent gates need that person's decision all the same: the ratification of a brief, the acceptance of a decision record or of a standing approval, the instruction to merge a named pull request or stack, and the answers to questions a run brings back. An Orchestrator session cannot tell a faithful relay of that person's words from any other agent message, and harness text holds none of that person's authority (ADR-D-0017). ADR-D-0038, accepted on 2026-10-01, therefore let a relay count only after an act of that person's own: a statement in that Orchestrator session, or an accepted admitting entry. The acceptance of that entry was itself such an act, so the first time in a repository that person opened an Orchestrator session to give it. The brief "One conversation with Counsel" has that person never open a second session, the first time in a repository included, and has Counsel start the session that sets a repository up, where nobody is at the window. The fork is whether that person's answer to the admitting entry is waited for in the window of a session that person did not open, or is carried there by Counsel before anything has admitted Counsel's relays.

## Decision

- An Orchestrator session takes a decision of the person directing the work from that person's own statement in that session, or from a relay by Counsel that quotes that person's words. Nothing else carries one: not Counsel's paraphrase, summary or own view, not any other agent's message, and not a line in a file, which records an act and is not the act.
- A relay counts only once admitted, in one of two ways: that person has told that Orchestrator session directly to accept Counsel's relays as that person's own; or the admitting entry, accepted by that person, is in effect and every Orchestrator session reads it at start. Without either, a relay is an agent's message and carries nothing, and the decision waits for that person's own statement, with the one exception that follows.
- In a session Counsel started, that person's answer to the admitting entry itself, yes or no, may be carried by Counsel as a relay quoting that person's words, and is taken on Counsel's word that that person said it. Nothing else is admitted before the entry is in effect: no other decision, no answer given without a quotation, and no relay in a session that was not told at its start that Counsel started it.
- A relay carries exactly what the quoted words say. The terms of each gate are unchanged, and the quoted words must themselves meet them, such as naming the pull request or the stack, the record, or the entry.
- What a relay rests on is named to that person and is what accepting the admitting entry takes on: an Orchestrator session cannot verify that Counsel quotes faithfully, who sent a message, or what its first message says of who started it. Every relayed decision therefore rests on the word of whoever writes as Counsel, and with this record so does the relay that carries the answer to the entry, with no act of that person's in that session before it.
- Approval or waiver of a plan is not carried by relay: plans are presented only by the Orchestrator session, and the record on plan approval governs where approval comes from (ADR-D-0064).
- A runtime's own check may refuse the write that puts the admitting entry in effect on a relayed word. No such check is got past (`ADR-D-a-session-counsel-started-does-not-stop-for-permission-in-its-own-window.md`): the entry stays not in effect, Counsel is told, and that person's own word in that session's window is what remains. That word in the window is a means of satisfying the runtime's check where the check requires it. It is no part of what this record decides the harness should do: what is decided is that the answer travels by Counsel, and wherever a runtime does not require the word in the window, none is asked for.

## Why

The product philosophy's "Who it is for" calls a design wrong that works only if the person understands a role or a step of the process, and opening a session of the other role once per repository, to type an acceptance into it, is such a step; the brief's statement, accepted on 2026-11-13, is that that person "never opens a second session, the first time in a repository included", the acceptance of the admitting entry being "given to Counsel and carried by Counsel". An act of that person's own in the Orchestrator session was what made Counsel's quoting the chosen carrier and not an agent's claim; it added no means of verifying any later relay, so what it protected was the first step only, and the price was the second session. With the answer carried, the same unverifiable word is relied on one step earlier, which is why the risk is named to that person and limited to the one answer. That person asked on 2026-11-13 that the handling be tried before it was fixed: "test them out so that it doesn't say something that turns out impossible in practice." The trial showed a runtime's check sometimes refusing the write, and on 2026-11-14 that person said of the word in the window that then remains: "One visit is acceptable, but not as a deliberate design choice rather is a means to satisfy the mechanisms at play."

## Rejected Alternatives

- The person directing the work states every decision in the Orchestrator session: it lost because that person talks with Counsel, to whom the Orchestrator's questions go, and should not need to type into another session; reopen if a supported runtime's instructions refuse every agent message as consent even after relays are admitted, or if a relayed quote is found to differ from what was said.
- That person's answer to the admitting entry is always given in the window of the Orchestrator session, the first time in a repository: it lost because it asks of that person once per repository the knowledge of roles and sessions the harness exists to spare them, and the statement so given verifies no later relay; reopen if a relay carrying an answer to the entry is found to have carried words that person did not say.
- Any message from Counsel counts as the word of the person directing the work: rejected outright; an agent's message would claim that person's authority (ADR-D-0017).
- In a session Counsel started, every relay counts from the first message, the admitting entry or not: rejected outright; the entry is where that person is shown what relays rest on and takes it on, and a session that acted on relays without it would have nothing of that person's to point to.
- A session that was not told at its start that Counsel started it takes the answer to the entry by relay as well: it lost because such a session may have a person at its window, who can be asked there; reopen if sessions are found waiting for an answer nobody is there to give.
- Counsel relays the substance in its own words: rejected outright; a paraphrase is Counsel's statement, not that person's.
- A ratified status written in a brief file counts as ratification: rejected outright; ratifying is an act of the person directing the work, and a line in a file records the act and is not it.
- The write that puts the entry in effect is repeated or made by another route until the runtime's check lets it pass: rejected outright; that would be getting past a check made for that person's protection.

## Decision Boundary

Not covered: statements of the person directing the work on channels other than the Orchestrator session and Counsel's relay, for example a pull-request comment from that person's account, which this record neither admits nor excludes; how the product owner's answer reaches the person directing the work, which precedes the relay; a standing approval in effect, which records an acceptance already given and is governed by the record on the value audit (ADR-D-0052), the sentence on a line in a file being about answers and consents given in the session; how a relay or an escalation travels between sessions, and how a session is told that Counsel started it; the wording of the admitting entry, the scope given to it, and what puts it in effect; what a session does with a proposed entry on a declined answer; how a runtime identifies the sender of a message; which checks a runtime makes, and permission checks other than the one on the write that puts the entry in effect (`ADR-D-a-session-counsel-started-does-not-stop-for-permission-in-its-own-window.md`); how the Orchestrator records a relay; what each consent gate requires, which its own record or rule states; whether an audit's verdict can authorize a plan, which this record does not grant; who starts a session and when (`ADR-D-a-ratified-briefs-run-starts-without-the-person-opening-anything.md`); a session that person opened by their own choice, where that person's own statement is available as before.

## Validation

The decision is broken where an Orchestrator session acts on something as a decision of the person directing the work that is neither that person's own statement in that session nor Counsel's relay quoting that person; where a relay is acted on with nothing having admitted it, the answer to the admitting entry in a session Counsel started excepted; where, before the entry is in effect, a session acts on a relay that carries anything but that answer, or on that answer without that person's quoted words, or in a session that was not told at its start that Counsel started it; where quoted words that do not meet a gate's terms are taken as meeting them; where a plan is approved or its approval waived on a relay; where an admitting entry is in effect that does not name what relays rest on; where the entry is in effect after a runtime's check refused the write and that person gave no word in that session; where that person is asked for a word in a session's window that no runtime's check required; or where, the first time in a repository, that person had to open a session in order to answer the entry.

## Revisit When

- What was seen of the answer to the entry carried by relay changes. On 2026-11-13, in Claude Code under its automatic permission mode, in sessions started for setup, with stand-ins for Counsel and for that person and made-up words: a quoted acceptance was acted on once, the entry put in effect and committed, and once the runtime's safety check refused the write and the session stopped and tried nothing else; an acceptance without a quotation was not acted on, twice; a quoted acceptance in a session not told that Counsel started it was not acted on, once. On that date a quoted decline was not acted on under wording that admitted an acceptance only, and under wording that admits it none had been carried; nor had a real Counsel session with that person's own words, a session started for a ratified brief, a relay over a peer channel, or any runtime but Claude Code been tried. An answer that cannot be carried so in a supported runtime, or one acted on that this record does not admit, reopens it.
- The premise that the runtime's check refuses the write only sometimes, and that that person's own word in the window satisfies it, fails. How often the check refuses was not measured: one write allowed and one refused on 2026-11-13. On the same date, in one case that was not the answer to the entry, an edit to an Orchestrator session's standing rules that the check had refused on a relayed brief went through unchanged after that person's word in that session's window; that word after a refusal of the write for the entry had not been tried on 2026-11-14. A runtime where the check always refuses makes the word in the window the rule there, and the second rejected alternative is then what happens in fact; a runtime that no longer refuses, or that lets that person's grant reach a started session without its window, removes the word in the window there with no change to this record.
- On 2026-09-30 a Claude Code Orchestrator session running Claude Fable 5.1 honoured Counsel's relays for value rulings after the statement of the person directing the work in that session, and on 2026-10-01 the same session honoured relayed acceptances of decision records. On 2026-11-11 that person's acceptance of a pull request by name ("I accept ADR-D-0071 and ADR-D-0072, and pull request 96."), relayed by Counsel and admitted by the admitting entry alone, was acted on in a session started after the entry was accepted, and the pull request was merged. On 2026-11-14 neither a relayed merge instruction nor a relay admitted by the entry alone had been exercised on any runtime but the one that session ran in; a failure of either reopens this record.
- A runtime whose instructions refuse every agent message as consent, even after relays are admitted, makes the relay unusable there; the decision then waits for that person's own statement, and the alternative of stating every decision in the Orchestrator session is reopened for that runtime.
- A relayed quote is found to differ from what the person directing the work said, or a message is found to have carried the Counsel identity, or the statement that Counsel started a session, without coming from Counsel.
- A runtime offers a channel that delivers that person's own messages from one session to another, or lets a session verify who sent a message, which makes the relay redundant or removes what it rests on.
