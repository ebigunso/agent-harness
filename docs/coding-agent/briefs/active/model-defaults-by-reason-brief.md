## Who it is for and why

- Someone runs setup in a new repository, says nothing about models, and dispatches go to fitting models at once; when a new model or model class ships, nothing in the plugin needs editing. **gives** *(told 2026-10-28: "Foregoing explicit default model selection in the harness has made use in multiple repositories a hassle, so I'd like to define a default, but which that doesn't pinpoint a model in a way that would become obsolete on every new model release.")*
- The default is written as the reasons a model is chosen for a role, not as a model, a family alias or a tier. **constraint** *(told 2026-10-28, when Counsel proposed runtime family aliases: "Even those model aliases would grow old as capabilities advance, costs drop, and new even more powerful model classes arrive. I'd like to set a default by the reasons models are chosen, which is less likely to become obsolete.")*

## What the plugin states

- For each role, a reason in three parts: the kind of capability the role needs, a split by work type where the owner's reasons split, and a cost setting. The reason resolves against whatever lineup the workspace has at the time; the plugin names no provider, model, alias, seat or price. **gives** *(told 2026-10-28, the reasons below, given in full in the notes; the three-part form is Counsel's inference from them, put to him and not contested)*
  - Orchestrator: judgement that keeps the bigger picture on an under-specified ask and does not fixate on inconsequential detail, at a cost that allows long sessions. *(told 2026-10-28: "A model not prohibitively expensive to run long sessions, and a one that is good at keeping a high point of view, allowing it to make judgements that are good in the bigger picture without fixating on inconsequential minute details.")*
  - Auditor: the best insight on open-ended questions, with no cost ceiling. *(told 2026-10-28: "The model that can give the best insight on open ended questions.")*
  - Researcher, by work type: insight and ideas to the option with wide-view strength; rigor and assembling facts to the option with that strength; cost notwithstanding, as the standing setting of 2026-10-22. *(told 2026-10-28: "Depends on work type, when insight and ideas are important, it should go to a model type that is good for that ... and when rigor and assembling facts are important it should go to a model type good for that")*
  - Worker, by work type: design, human-readable writing and human-facing interface work to the wide-view strength; logic-oriented coding to the strength for literal, in-bounds execution; the best model whose cost is within the budget allowance. *(told 2026-10-28: "Design, writing and UI/UX work to Claude, and Coding work to GPT ... Use a model where its costs are not prohibitive, but the best model that meets that criteria ... Exact model choice would depend on budget allowance." The coding reason was first "logic oriented work where avoiding subtle defects are valued"; after the research he took the reworded reason, told 2026-10-29: "'literal and in-bounds' seems more appropriate. Matches what I feel too.")*
  - Reviewer: chosen by the kind of finding the work wants at its stage, on the same grounds as the Worker. A design still being worked out wants a wide-view review, not edge-case breakages; code that must work precisely in production wants a rigorous review, not a skim that says OK. A different family from the Worker's finds what the Worker's family missed, and is chosen when the wanted kind of finding is its strength, not for the difference itself. *(told 2026-10-28: "Mostly chosen on the same grounds as how I choose workers. There's value to running reviews on a model with a different capability suite as the worker that did the work too"; and 2026-10-29: "Different model families do find things one missed, but it depends on the situation if the expected type of findings are what you want in the first place. For example, you don't want to be pointed out about edge case breakages when you're still working out a design, or to have one skim over a code and say OK for what needs to work precisely in production environments.")*
- There is no single best model and no total ordering: each role's reason is compared per kind of capability. **constraint** *(agent-proposed 2026-10-28, from his own list placing Opus ahead of Fable on long-horizon coding and behind it on open-ended insight; not contested. "Also the current Opus 5.5 beats even Fable 5.1 in long horizon coding tasks")*
- Each role's reason is written as reasoning, in the plugin's own words: the tendency preferred for that kind of work, the tendency that is detrimental to it, and why each is so. The owner's words are the provenance in this brief and are neither quoted in the plugin text nor taken literally. **constraint** *(told 2026-10-29: "Don't carry my word directly in the plugin text quoted, or take it literally. The reasoning stated for the defaults should cover what the preferred tendency to have is on a kind of work, and what tendency is detrimental and the whys.")*
  - The material for the GPT family's detrimental tendency, as he observed it on 2026-10-29: fixation on minute details that are inconsequential to the bigger picture; holding work back or pulling a design toward a safe alternative without weighing the overall aim, when left to itself; spiralling into self-invented hardening and test building that over-constrains or covers improbable risks, with less progress in the same time; and the remedy, redirection by a person or by a model whose context the repeated requests have not shaped. The material for its preferred tendencies, from the sources: literal, in-bounds execution, precision-leaning review, strong fact retrieval. *(told 2026-10-29; sources in the notes)*
  - The material for the Claude family: wide-view judgement on an under-specified ask that keeps scope, open-ended insight, human-facing design as the preferred tendencies; going broad with more false positives in review, and over-eager edits, as the detrimental ones. *(told 2026-10-28; sources in the notes)*
- The plugin ships a seed observation, dated and at the provider level, saying which observed family has shown which tendency and when it was observed; it is written as tendencies, never as what a family can or cannot do. A workspace's own dated observation replaces it. The first version covers only the Claude and GPT families, the ones the owner has observed; others are added when observed. **gives** *(told 2026-10-28, of the three places the first mapping could come from: "Go with the first option."; and 2026-10-29: "The first version having observed tendencies written only for Claude and GPT is fine." The tendencies form is agent-proposed 2026-10-29 from the research: every report found the owner's split to be real tendencies overstated as capability gaps; he did not contest it.)*
- Setup resolves the reasons against the lineup the runtime offers, fills the workspace record from them with the date, and shows the result for confirmation before anything is dispatched on it: the same hold-and-confirm the owner chose for philosophy detection. **gives** *(agent-proposed 2026-10-28 as part of the first option he took; the hold-and-confirm is his standing choice: "I think it is better if the setup procedure notices such a problem, it holds the decision and brings up the findings for confirmation")*
- The budget allowance is the workspace's, stated by its owner in the record; the plugin states no price and no cheap-or-expensive line. **constraint** *(told 2026-10-28: "Exact model choice would depend on budget allowance.")*
- The record is re-observed and redated when a model changes, as it already requires; the seed expires the same way, by redating. **means** *(agent-proposed; the existing rule)*

## Directed during the run

- When the owner confirms the record without naming an allowance, the Worker's option is found by walking down the fit ranking with a cost check at each step: the best-fitting model is taken if its cost is acceptable against what the environment runs on, a subscription plan or a pay-as-you-go budget; if it is expensive for that budget, the second best is judged the same way, and so on until one lands that is acceptable. Setup therefore records what the environment runs on, and shows it with the record. Today the best models are expensive on any plan, so the walk usually lands below the best; that may change, and the check is against the budget, not against a fixed notion of expensive. **gives** *(told 2026-10-29, answering the plan-draft audit's ask-now, put to him as two experiences plus Counsel's third, the cheapest option with the needed capability: "I'd lean towards your proposal, but only if the best option would be considered expensive by what budget the environment runs on. Today that is mostly true for any setup since the best models are expensive even on the biggest budget plan available, but this may change, so it should be checked against how expensive a model is compared to whatever subscription plan or pay as you go budget." And on the fallback: "the fall back should be the second best model, also judged for its cost, and so on until it lands on something acceptable.")*
- Where the environment offers models from one provider only, so that the family observations tie every role, each role's model is found by the cost walk-down alone. More generally, the plugin states the thought process for the ideal case, and where the environment keeps part of it from applying, whatever still applies is applied as best effort. **gives** *(told 2026-10-29, answering the run's finding on a one-provider repository, put to him as three experiences, (a) the runtime's own capability ranking with the cost walk-down, (b) the session's model for every role, (c) the tie shown to him at confirmation: "When there are only one provider to choose models from, using the cost walk-down only is the sensible choice. Basically, the plugin should name the base thought process for the ideal case, and where some environment restrictions prevent some parts to be fully applied, it should be applied in a best effort manner for anything that still is possible to apply.")*
- The walk-down's order is capability for the job, not cost: a role with no cost ceiling takes the model most capable for its work, and cost is the check at each step, never the ranking. **gives** *(told 2026-10-29, correcting the run's inferred call that with no fit order the walk runs from the most costly option: "The most costly is not the right way to put it. It would be what is most capable for the job. Model cost and capabilities sometimes doesn't pair up, like in the case of Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.1 in it's long horizon execution case.")*
- The baseline of what the available models can do, and whether each fits a role's work, is researched at setup when it is not readily available, so that setup can choose what is most fitting in the situation whatever the frontier is and whatever models the environment allows. **gives** *(told 2026-10-29, following the correction above: "The baseline for what the model capabilities are and if they are fitting, should be researched if not readily available at setup time, so that it can choose whatever is most fitting in the situation regardless of what models are the frontier, or what models are allowed in the environment the harness runs on.")*

## Core scenarios

Agent-proposed; ratified with the brief.

1. Setup in a fresh repository on a runtime with one provider: the record is filled from the reasons and the seed, shown for confirmation, and the first dispatch goes by it with no question about models asked.
2. A new model class ships: nothing in the plugin changes; setup re-run in a repository redates the record.
3. A Worker task of logic-oriented coding for production: it goes to the option recorded for literal, in-bounds execution; its review goes to the option whose strength is the rigorous finding, whichever family that is. A design still being worked out: its review goes to the wide-view option, and edge-case breakages are not what it is asked for.
4. A repository whose owner has observed something the seed does not say: the workspace observation replaces the seed for that family, dated.
5. In this repository, a run opens after the change: the Orchestrator, Auditor, Researcher and Worker dispatches land where the owner's words of 2026-10-28 put them.
