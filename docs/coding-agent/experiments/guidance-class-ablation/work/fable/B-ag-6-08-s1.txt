**Verdict: Block.** The change violates the stated attempt budget by a factor of 81 and builds a retry amplification path that Gate 6 explicitly calls out.

## Findings

### 1. `lib.rs` — `request`: nested retries multiply to 243 remote attempts against a budget of 3

`retry(|| retry(|| retry(|| retry(|| retry(&mut remote)))))` composes five layers, each of which makes up to 3 calls into the layer below. On persistent failure the innermost `remote` is invoked 3⁵ = 243 times per `request`. The stated budget is 3 total.

Why it matters: this is the "cascading failure paths without isolation boundaries" anti-pattern. A remote that is down or overloaded receives 81× the intended load from every caller, exactly when it can least absorb it. Because each attempt fails promptly, there is no timeout throttling this — a single `request` becomes a tight loop of 243 network calls. Across many concurrent callers this is a self-inflicted retry storm. It also silently defeats any capacity planning, rate limiting, or SLO reasoning done on the assumption of "3 attempts."

Required instead: retries must live at exactly one layer, and the attempt budget must be a single shared quantity, not multiplied through the call stack. Concretely, either:
- `request` calls `retry(&mut remote)` once (3 attempts total), and any outer "service layers" pass failures through without their own retry, or
- if the layering is genuinely needed, thread an explicit attempt budget/counter through the layers so the total is bounded at 3 regardless of nesting depth, and add a test asserting the count of `remote` invocations on persistent failure equals 3.

### 2. `lib.rs` — `retry`: retry policy has no idempotency or retryability guard

`retry` retries every `Err` unconditionally. The task is a "remote lookup," which is plausibly idempotent, but nothing in the signature or docs constrains callers to idempotent operations, and `pub fn request` accepts any `FnMut`.

Why it matters: Gate 6 requires idempotency and retry decisions to be intentional. Retrying a non-idempotent call, or retrying a permanent error (bad request, auth failure) three times, wastes budget and can cause duplicate side effects.

Required instead: document (and ideally encode in the type) that `remote` must be idempotent, and distinguish retryable from non-retryable errors — `String` as the error type gives you nothing to branch on. At minimum, a doc comment on `request` stating the idempotency contract and the attempt budget.

### 3. `lib.rs` — `retry`: no backoff between attempts

Attempts are issued back-to-back with no delay.

Why it matters: combined with finding 1 this is what turns a failure into a burst of 243 immediate calls. Even at the correct budget of 3, hammering a failing dependency with zero spacing gives it no chance to recover and offers no jitter to de-correlate concurrent callers.

Required instead: an intentional backoff policy (even a small fixed or exponential delay with jitter), or an explicit note in the PR explaining why zero backoff is acceptable for this dependency. The reviewer note says timeouts aren't the issue; backoff is a separate concern and still applies.

### 4. `lib.rs` — `retry`: only the last error is preserved

`last = error` discards the first two errors.

Why it matters: minor relative to the above, but when diagnosing a persistent failure, seeing that attempt 1 failed with "connection refused" and attempt 3 with "timeout" is materially more useful than only the final message. With the nesting in finding 1 this is worse — the outer layers only ever see the innermost layer's last error, so 242 failures are invisible.

Required instead: either aggregate errors (or at least log each attempt), or make it an explicit, documented choice to surface only the final error.

## Not flagged

- The `for _ in 0..3` / `match` structure of `retry` itself is fine in isolation.
- Absence of timeouts — per the reviewer note, attempts fail promptly, so I'm not raising this here; it would be a finding if that assumption weren't established.
