## Goal
- The harness gives what `docs/coding-agent/briefs/active/design-led-long-runs-brief.md` states: a design held in a brief is carried over as many plans as it takes without the owner, the run keeps itself to the value documents and stops when it stops getting closer, a better design noticed mid-flight reaches the owner while the affected part waits, independent judgement of the Orchestrator's work is a role of its own, and Counsel's policy is written to give the brief's core scenarios 5 to 9, which stay the owner's to judge in first use.
- The brief is the requirement and is not restated here; where this plan and the brief differ, the brief governs and the difference is a plan defect.
- This is the first plan of the run. It carries the brief form, Counsel's discussion and the Auditor role, and proposes the one decision record that role needs. The run across plans itself (the brief's "The run", "Keeping to the philosophy without you", "A better design noticed mid-flight" and "Closeout") is the work of the run's second plan, drafted when this one closes; the Definition of Done below marks which items are this plan's.

## Definition of Done
- The brief form states the kind marks (gives, constraint, means), that a design lives in the brief, the core scenarios ratified with the brief, and that a philosophy says what its owner ratified without having to be in that person's wording, Counsel-originated statements marked as such; a brief written before the marks existed is still readable by the audit.
- Counsel's policy covers the brief's scenarios 5 to 9: it adds what 5, 6 and 8 lack (starting from nothing; weighing an idea against the philosophy and the repository and naming what was missed or a better way; saying when an implementation detail would constrain the build prematurely), keeps what already gives 7 and 9 (explaining unfamiliar ground first; the closing pass), and the closing pass defines the core scenarios with the owner.
- The Auditor is a logical role with an adapter in each runtime, stated with its home in the role map; the value audit and the goal-mode assessment are dispatched to it; the Reviewer keeps plan review, wave and final review and its evidence duties; both fixed dispatch templates are byte-identical to before.
- (second plan) A run under a brief spans as many plan-mode plans as the design takes, each authorized by the Plan Gate as it stands; `candidate ready` and the note to Counsel come once, at the end of the run; the run counts demonstrated scenarios, and a run that has stopped getting closer stops and tells the owner.
- (second plan) The run keeps itself to the value documents and corrects itself while the work is ongoing, the owner hearing only when it could not. The brief's means for this is built from: before each audit the Orchestrator commits its own reading of every item; the audit compares it with its own grades; a divergence is corrected inside the run and a repeated one stops the affected part as a value question. No remedy refreshes the Orchestrator's context (a constraint).
- (second plan) A design-level finding noticed during the work is recorded and reaches the owner whatever acting on it would cost; a trivial one never does; a plan that departs from a means is such a finding and not a breach. The part it concerns waiting while the rest continues is the brief's means and is built from.
- (second plan) Closeout of a run leads with behaviour scenario by scenario with how to observe each; updating the durable design document for the field the work touched is the brief's means and is carried as a closeout step.
- Goal mode for goals a check can decide behaves as it does today: its references change only where they name who is dispatched for the assessment.
- Every decision record this work proposes is accepted by the owner by name before it lands; package validators pass; each branch that will become a pull request has Reviewer `APPROVED`; nothing merges before the owner has judged the stack.
- (run closeout, after the second plan) Scenarios 1 to 4 are demonstrated by a run where one can be made, and where one cannot the closeout says so plainly.

## Design
- Chosen: a run is a sequence of ordinary plan-mode plans under one brief, tied together by a run record. Each plan goes through the Plan Gate, its review and its audits unchanged; what is new sits around them: the record, the plan/run split at closeout, the committed reading, the finding route, the scenario count and the stall rule. The Auditor is a fourth dispatched role whose adapters carry no procedure and point at the two existing mandates.
- Alternative: extend goal mode to goals a person judges, the brief as its envelope and the assessor as its stall judge.
- Lenses. structure: chosen adds one artifact (the run record) and one role, with no new dependency between modes; the alternative couples value-level operation to goal mode and gives one loop two kinds of end state. evolution: chosen replaces one goal-mode record (ADR-D-0030, on who runs the assessment) and leaves the others standing; the alternative supersedes ADR-D-0027 and rewrites the admission test, a larger blast radius, and the brief's stated direction is that goal mode for checkable goals keeps working as it does today (brief, "Limits"). verification: chosen is checked by reading the changed plan-mode references and by a goal-mode diff that shows only role names changed; the alternative has no such cheap check. operation: chosen costs one audit dispatch per position as today plus a comparison inside it; a stall is detected at a plan boundary, later than goal mode's per-iteration check. human: chosen keeps "plan" meaning what it means today and adds "run"; the alternative makes "goal" mean two things. safety: chosen changes no source of authorization and adds one stop (the stall) and one hold (a finding's part); the alternative would let a ratified brief authorize an envelope, which ADR-D-0040 reserves for a record of its own.
- Why chosen: it gives every gives-statement of the brief while changing the fewest accepted rules.
- Watch list touched, to be reported and not claimed clear: entry 2 (a change to how goal mode behaves today): the in-loop assessment is dispatched to the Auditor instead of the Reviewer, with its template and mandate unchanged, and assumption A1 decides which goal-mode checks stay with the Reviewer; entry 3 (who may authorize or stop work): no source of authorization changes and no stop is removed, but two are added (the audit-judged stall; the hold on a finding's part). Entry 1 (reading plans again) is not touched.
