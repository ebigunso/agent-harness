# Philosophy Reference

A gap check, read only after the discussion has produced a draft of a philosophy. It is not a form, a template or an outline, and it supplies no wording: the draft keeps the shape the discussion gave it. List what is below that the draft does not cover, as gaps for the person entitled to ratify it to fill through another round of discussion or to leave out.

## What a philosophy holds

1. A one-sentence thesis.
2. The scene and the measure: who, in what situation, experiences what; the single measure of success, and the failure that counts whatever else is right.
3. The core distinction or principles, few and named, each with its reason; where two goods conflict, which wins and why.
4. How it behaves, stated as what someone experiences, by analogy to a person where that helps.
5. What it aspires to: the legitimate future that extension is measured against, and the honest fallback until then.
6. What it must not be: refusals, each countering a comfortable default.
7. Success as observable behaviour: a short list one could watch for.
8. A north star: one question every decision is held to, and the tie-break when in doubt.
9. What is left out on purpose, and why.

## What is not in a philosophy

Architecture and the list of the system's parts, named patterns or methods, metrics, backend or vendor choices, and open design questions. Each belongs in a decision record with its context.

## Qualities checked

- Prose that gives a view to reason from.
- Every principle cuts both ways: what it refuses and what it demands.
- Conflicts between goods are settled in the text.
- A reason is present on every line.
- The comfortable defaults the document exists to counter are named as such.

## An example

Invented, for a made-up note-taking tool for field biologists; it shows the qualities, not a shape to copy.

> Field notes should survive the field. A biologist on a wet hillside, one hand free, records what she sees and finds it months later at a desk, intact and in its context; that is the measure, and a note lost or silently altered is the failure that counts whatever else works. Capture comes before structure: the tool takes the note first and asks nothing it could ask later, because a question at the moment of capture costs the observation; where tidy data and fast capture conflict, capture wins and tidying waits for the desk. It behaves like a good field assistant, who writes down what you say and asks what you meant only afterwards. It is not a database wearing a notebook's face: the comfortable default is to demand fields up front, and it refuses that. When in doubt, ask whether it would still work with cold fingers in the rain.
